# TIM.EXE 1.11 - The Even More Incredible Machine

The reconstruction's target since 2026-09-30. 1.00 (`incredible-machine/`) is
reconstructed and tagged `tim-1.00`; this is what is known about 1.11, the
executable GOG ships as The Even More Incredible Machine (installer 2.1.0.24,
extracted into `even-more-incredible-machine/`, which is not tracked).

## The file

`TIM.EXE`, 114,652 bytes, dated 17 Nov 1993. The data files are dated 26 Jul
1993 and the level count is 160 (`L1.LEV`..`L160.LEV` are probed; 1.00 probes
87). The executable says "This is version 1.11 of 'The Incredible Machine.'".

## Recovering the image: `tools/unrnc.py`

1.11 is **RNC ProPack method 1, twice, both layers keyed** - not LZEXE. The
outer layer is 117,412 bytes unpacked and holds the inner layer and its DOS
stub; the inner layer is the program, 220,992 bytes (0x35f40), which is its
whole memory image: the stack at `35ec:0080` ends exactly there.

Running the stubs under the emulator, as `tools/unlzexe.py` does for 1.00,
does not work: both come out with output that changes with the load address
and matches no CRC. The stub makes no DOS call, so it is the CPU emulation
that goes wrong somewhere; it was not chased. `tools/unrnc.py` decodes the
format instead and is held to RNC's own CRCs, which both layers pass:

- the key is the word after the 18-byte header, with bit 1 the "keyed" flag:
  0x664e is key 0x664c (outer), 0xfffe is 0xffff (inner);
- literal bytes are XORed with the key's low byte; the key is rotated right
  twice before the first run and once after every non-empty run;
- the bit stream starts after the key word, and its first two bits are
  skipped.

The entry (`0000:0000`), the stack and the **2,758 relocations** come out of
the inner stub: the fields at its cs:0, and the table at its cs:0x29a that the
stub walks - a byte count (0 ends), a segment word, then that many byte-sized
steps of the offset. Every relocated word is a segment inside the image; the
largest is 0x2fe1, DGROUP's.

## The layout

DGROUP is at 0x2fe10 (1.00: 0x2d3c0): the code is about 10.8 KB larger and
the initialised data about 4.9 KB smaller. The C startup and the copyright
string are the same.

## RESOURCE.CFG

**1.11's TIM.EXE reads RESOURCE.CFG as 1.00 does: three bytes**, the first
ignored, then the music device and the digitised module. GOG ships a
181-byte *text* file (`soundDrv = SBPRO.DRV`, `audioDrv = AUDBLAST.DRV`, ...)
in the format of Sierra's driver loader, which TIM.EXE does not read as text:
it takes " so" as device 0x73 and module 0x6f, past the ends of both tables.
Under the emulator that led the Sound Blaster module's IRQ probe to restore
IRQ 0's vector from a copy it never saved, which zeroed INT 8's vector; the
game's timer handler then chained to 0000:0000.

A copy of the game directory with a three-byte RESOURCE.CFG (`00 00 fe`: PC
speaker, no module) runs: the title (flip 120) and the credits (flip 450)
draw, 475 flips in 250M instructions. The `.DRV` names in the image's strings
(`STD.DRV`, `SBPRO.DRV`, `AUDBLAST.DRV`, ...) and the `SoundDrv`, `VideoDrv`,
`MouseDrv`, `JoyDrv`, `MemoryDrv` names are what 1.11 knows; which of them the
game loads, and from where, is not established yet - a run opens `VM.OVL` and
`SX.OVL` (from the resource archive) and no `.DRV` file.

## How far 1.00's source is from it

Matched routine by routine (1.00's 1,157 public routines from the link map,
immediates, displacements, call targets and relocated words masked), in
TEMIM's image:

| | routines |
| --- | --- |
| byte-identical, moved | 160 |
| same instructions, other addresses and constants | 354 |
| the same logic compiled differently | ~570 |
| no good match | 41 |
| too short to place | 29 |

"Compiled differently" is mostly code generation, not game changes: in
`part_hit_bellow` the logic and the record offsets are the same, and 1.11
merges two identical tails and returns early, which is jump optimisation
(`-O`) or a later compiler. It is spread over every segment, part kinds
included. The 41 are mostly the compressors' writers (`lzss_flush`,
`lzw_flush`, the Huffman encoder, `insert_node`/`delete_node`), and
`part_handle_at_pointer`, `part_key_shortcut`, `settle_carried_part` and some
sound routines.

New in 1.11, from its strings: "ADJUST PARTS BIN", "CLEAR PARTS BIN" with a
confirmation, "(Click here to enter description)", a data-disk check, the
named driver types above, `dynamix.pal`/`dynamix.scr` in place of `sierra.*`,
`gp_go.bmp`, and 560K of free memory where 1.00 wants 550K.

## Where the sources stand

**Addresses.** `tools/addrmap.py` places each of 1.00's routines (read from
the sources as tagged `tim-1.00`) in 1.11: exactly, with every constant,
displacement, call target and relocated word masked, or else by resemblance -
and a resemblance may only land on **an entry of 1.11's**: a far call's
target, a near call's, a relocated far pointer to a `push bp / mov bp, sp`,
or a prologue straight after a return. Without that rule it placed a routine
at whichever instruction looked best, which put `draw_frame_corners`,
`game_teardown`, `copy_rect_around_cursor` and `select_cursor` a few bytes
into the routine before theirs; with it all four land where 1.11's callers
call. A placement is taken (`--apply`) when it is exact or resembles at 0.6
or better: 1,051 addresses in 53 files. 163 keep their 1.00 address and say
"(1.00's; not yet placed in 1.11)" beside it. Addresses mentioned in prose,
the image ranges in the file headers and the `JUDGE: data` bounds are still
1.00's: data needs a map of its own.

**Compiler options.** 1.11's modules were rebuilt with **`-O -Z`** - jump
optimisation and redundant-load suppression - on top of 1.00's options.
Measured file by file with 1.00's sources (every routine judged with the
file's own options, then with `-O`, then with `-Z` added):

| | routines matching |
| --- | --- |
| 1.00's options | 339 of 1,079 |
| `-O` added | 433 |
| `-O -Z` added | 531 |

`game.c` goes from 3 of 57 to 51, `machine.c` from 13 of 75 to 50. The new
key filter at 0x0905a (below) matches only with `-O`. Three files do
*worse* with `-O` - `fstring.c` (7 of 7 without it), `mono.c` (6 of 6) and
`rects.c` - and keep 1.00's options: their objects look reused unchanged.
`resource.c` too. The modules 1.00 built with `-G -O` did not move either
way. Where `-O` or `-Z` tied, the file takes them, as the build as a whole
did; each is a measurement to revisit when its routines are transcribed for
1.11. `-d` is gone from `intro.c`: its pool holds "corners.bmp" twice.

**The tools read the version from the image.** `tim.DGROUPS` gives each
version's DGROUP by the size of its recovered image, and `disasm.py` and the
judge take theirs from it. `tools/runtime_names.json` is regenerated for 1.11
by turboc's `libmatch.py` (82 names, `__ftoupper` new); 1.00's are kept
beside them as `names_1.00`. `tools/datamap.py` places 1.00's DGROUP objects
in 1.11 the way addrmap places routines.

**`intro.c` is the first module proven against 1.11**: all six routines and
its `_DATA` match, protected and cracked. 1.11 draws the Dynamix screen
(`jtp.bmp`, `dynamix.scr`, `dynamix.pal`) on a border of colour 6 - the video
driver's slot 25, `vm_set_border_colour`, which 1.00 carried a thunk for and
never called; VM.OVL is byte for byte 1.00's - holds its last frame for 0x78
ticks, preloads sounds by how much memory there is (`g_preload_sound_ids`,
`g_preload_sound_need`, `g_memory_k`), loads the panel art itself
(`newcp.bmp`, `newgpbor.bmp`), plays the credits' tunes 0x3ea..0x3fd, and
shuffles the copy-protection grid through a table.

**A key filter**, new at 0x0905a, stands between `bios_read_key` and every
screen's key: `g_last_key` (DGROUP 0x4ef5) is now the whole word it returns,
scancode and character, and the screens test `(g_last_key & 0x7f) == 9` for
Tab where 1.00 kept one byte.

## The copy protection

GOG's 1.11 is cracked, but not as the 1.00 copy was. `copy_protect_screen`
still asks and still waits; after the third pick, where the original starts
the slots over -

    0fcf4  ff46f2         inc  word [bp-0xe]        slot++
    0fcf7  837ef203       cmp  word [bp-0xe], 3
    0fcfb  7505           jne  0x0fd02
    0fcfd  c746f20000     mov  word [bp-0xe], 0     slot = 0

- the crack wrote `eb 51` over the `mov`'s first two bytes and `00` over its
third: `jmp 0x0fd50`, which is `done = 1`. So any three parts pass, on the
third click. The two zero bytes after it are the `mov`'s immediate, left
behind. The original bytes are 1.00's same instruction (`c7 46 f0 00 00` at
0x0ed8a) with 1.11's slot offset. `tools/uncrack.py`'s `CRACKS` holds each
version's crack by the size of its image, and the judge and `tools/link.py`
put 1.11's three bytes back, as they do 1.00's one, and build with
`TIM_COPY_PROTECTION`. The source's default is the crack - `goto check`
after the third pick, and the two bytes it left behind as `asm db` - which
the judge's `--cracked` proves against the shipped bytes.
